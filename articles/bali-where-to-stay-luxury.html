Cliffs, jungle or beach club

Where to stay in Bali
for a luxury trip

Bali rewards travelers who choose two bases well and stop trying to see the whole island from one.

GoCart Global editors · Updated 2026-10-06 · How we choose

This guide is for travelers planning a high-end Bali trip who want to know which part of the island fits them. The bottom line: split your stay between the coast and Ubud. Base yourself on the Bukit Peninsula around Uluwatu for cliff-top drama, surf and sunsets, or in Nusa Dua for calm, family-friendly beaches. Then spend at least three nights in Ubud's river valleys for rice terraces, temples and the island's cultural heart. Seminyak and Canggu suit those who want restaurants, beach clubs and shopping on the doorstep; Jimbaran and Sanur offer quieter, easy-going shorelines. Traffic is the hidden variable, so choose bases rather than day-tripping from one, and travel in the dry season, April to October.

Uluwatu and the Bukit Peninsula: who is it for?

The Bukit is the limestone peninsula at Bali's southern tip, and it looks nothing like the rice-field postcards. Sheer cliffs drop to turquoise water, with small beaches reached by steps cut into the rock — Padang Padang, Bingin and Suluban among them — and world-class surf breaks below.

Luxury here means cliff-top resorts and private pool villas designed around the horizon and the sunset. It is the strongest choice for honeymooners, surfers and anyone who wants ocean views from every room. Uluwatu Temple, perched on a cliff edge, hosts a Kecak fire dance at sunset that is worth one evening despite the crowds.

The trade-offs: beaches can involve a climb, waves are often too strong for casual swimming, and you will drive to most restaurants outside your resort. Families with young children may prefer the calmer water of Nusa Dua, a short drive away on the same peninsula.

Ubud: why the jungle deserves at least three nights

Ubud sits inland in the foothills, and it is where Bali's Hindu culture, crafts and landscape come into focus. The town itself is busy, but the luxury resorts lie outside it, hanging over the Ayung River gorge and terraced valleys, with infinity pools that seem to merge with the canopy.

Days here move at a different rhythm: an early walk along the Campuhan Ridge, rice terraces at Tegallalang or the broader UNESCO-listed subak landscapes of Jatiluwih further west, water-temple purification rituals, a pre-dawn hike up Mount Batur for sunrise, and spa treatments rooted in Balinese tradition. Evenings bring dance performances at palace and temple stages.

Ubud is cooler and more humid than the coast, with frequent afternoon showers even in drier months. It is not a beach destination, so pair it with a coastal base rather than treating it as a day trip — the drive from the south can be slow, and Ubud is at its best at dawn and dusk, when day-trippers are gone.

Nusa Dua, Seminyak, Canggu, Jimbaran or Sanur?

  • Nusa Dua — a planned, gated resort enclave on the Bukit's east side, with manicured grounds, reef-protected beaches and calm water. Excellent for families and for travelers who want everything handled; less local character.
  • Seminyak — the west coast's style district: boutiques, restaurants, cocktail bars and beach clubs along a broad sunset-facing beach. Best for social travelers who want to walk to dinner. Waves can be strong.
  • Canggu — younger and more casual, with surf breaks, cafés and villas among the remaining rice fields. Traffic is heavy and development dense.
  • Jimbaran — a sweeping bay close to the airport, known for seafood grilled on the sand at sunset. A gentle, convenient choice for first and last nights.
  • Sanur — the calm east coast, with a beachfront path, sunrise views and a relaxed, older crowd. It is also the departure point for fast boats to Nusa Penida and Nusa Lembongan.

How should you split a Bali stay?

For most luxury travelers, two bases over eight to ten nights is the sweet spot: four or five nights in Ubud, then four or five on the coast. Ending by the sea lets you unwind after the cultural days and puts you closer to the airport for departure.

Sample combinations:

  • Honeymoon: Ubud valley villa, then an Uluwatu cliff-top pool villa.
  • Family: Nusa Dua for the beach and kids' clubs, with a few nights in an Ubud resort that offers cultural activities for children.
  • Food and nightlife: Seminyak for restaurants and beach clubs, with Ubud as the quiet counterweight.
  • Longer trip: add east Bali — Sidemen's valleys or the black-sand coast near Amed — or the Nusa islands for cliffs and manta snorkeling.

Resist the urge to add a third or fourth coastal base. Each move costs a half-day of transit, and in Bali the joy is in settling in.

Getting around Bali

The international airport sits on the narrow isthmus south of Denpasar, close to Jimbaran, Kuta and the Bukit. From there, everything is by road, and traffic is the defining challenge, especially in the southwest between Seminyak, Canggu and the airport, and around Ubud town.

The standard approach for luxury travelers is a private car with a driver, arranged through your hotel or a reputable local operator, either for transfers or by the day. Drivers know the back roads, wait while you visit temples and often double as informal guides. Many resorts include airport transfers in suite or villa packages.

Tips:

  • Plan sightseeing by region rather than criss-crossing the island in one day.
  • Leave early — mornings are cooler and roads quieter.
  • Ride-hailing apps work in many areas, though some zones restrict pickups.
  • Self-driving a scooter is not recommended for visitors unfamiliar with local traffic.

When is the best time to visit Bali?

Bali has two seasons. The dry season, roughly April to October, brings sunny days, lower humidity and the best conditions for the west coast surf and beach time. July, August and the year-end holidays are the busiest periods, so book villas well ahead.

The wet season, roughly November to March, brings heavier rain, usually in afternoon or evening downpours, with lush green landscapes in between. It is a fine time for Ubud and spa-focused stays, and the island is quieter outside the Christmas period. East-coast surf breaks work better in these months.

One date to check: Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, usually in March. For a full day the island shuts down — no lights, no traffic, and the airport closes. Guests must stay within their hotel, which many visitors find remarkably peaceful. The evening before features ogoh-ogoh parades of giant effigies through the villages.

Temple etiquette and local customs

Bali's Hindu culture is woven into daily life, and you will see small woven offerings, called canang sari, on doorsteps, shrines and dashboards. Step around them rather than on them.

  • Wear a sarong and sash when entering temples; most sites lend or rent them at the gate, but carrying your own is more comfortable.
  • Cover shoulders at temples and be quiet during ceremonies; do not walk in front of people praying.
  • Use your right hand to give and receive items.
  • Avoid touching heads, which are considered sacred, including children's.
  • Ask before photographing people at ceremonies, and never climb on shrines or temple walls.

Ceremonies and processions can close roads unexpectedly; your driver will know how to work around them, and it is worth pausing to watch rather than hurrying past.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Is it better to stay in Uluwatu or Ubud?

They serve different purposes, so most luxury travelers do both. Uluwatu offers cliff-top views, surf and sunsets; Ubud offers jungle valleys, rice terraces and Balinese culture. If you must choose one, pick Uluwatu for a beach-focused honeymoon and Ubud for culture, wellness and scenery.

How many days do you need in Bali?

Eight to ten nights allows two well-chosen bases — typically Ubud and a coastal area — without rushing. A week works with one move. Longer trips can add east Bali or the Nusa islands. Because traffic is slow, fewer bases and longer stays make for a better experience.

Which area of Bali is best for families?

Nusa Dua is the easiest choice, with calm, reef-protected beaches, large resorts with kids' clubs and a secure, orderly setting. Sanur is another gentle option. Uluwatu's cliffs and strong surf are less suited to young children, while Ubud works well for a few nights of cultural activities.

What is the best month to visit Bali?

The dry season, roughly April to October, offers the most reliable sunshine. May, June and September are often considered ideal, balancing good weather with fewer crowds than July, August and the year-end holidays. The wet season brings afternoon downpours but lush scenery and quieter resorts.

What is Nyepi and how does it affect travelers?

Nyepi is the Balinese Day of Silence, usually falling in March. For roughly 24 hours the island shuts down: no traffic, lights or noise, and the airport closes. Visitors must remain in their hotel. Check the date before booking flights; many travelers enjoy the stillness and the parades the night before.

How do you get around Bali in luxury?

Hire a private car with a driver, either per transfer or by the day, through your hotel or a reputable operator. It is the most comfortable and efficient way to handle Bali's traffic, and drivers often act as local guides. Plan days by region to avoid long cross-island drives.

What should I wear to temples in Bali?

Wear a sarong tied at the waist with a sash, and cover your shoulders. Most temples lend or rent sarongs at the entrance. Speak quietly, follow any signs at the gate and avoid stepping on offerings placed on the ground.
