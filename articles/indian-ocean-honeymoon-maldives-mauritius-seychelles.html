Atolls, volcanic peaks or granite boulders

Maldives, Mauritius
or Seychelles?

Three Indian Ocean honeymoons that look alike on a screensaver and feel nothing alike in person.

GoCart Global editors · Updated 2026-10-06 · How we choose

This guide is for couples deciding which Indian Ocean island to choose for a honeymoon. The bottom line: the Maldives is for doing beautifully little on the water — overwater villas, reefs and total seclusion. Mauritius is for variety: a larger volcanic island with lagoon beaches, mountains, golf, food and culture to explore. The Seychelles is for dramatic natural beauty — granite boulders, rainforest and island-hopping between Mahé, Praslin and La Digue. Seasons differ: the Maldives is driest from December to April, Mauritius is pleasant most of the year outside its humid cyclone-risk summer, and the Seychelles is calmest in the transition months of April–May and October–November. Combining two is possible but takes planning.

Three islands, three personalities

The Maldives is a nation of low coral atolls spread across the equator. There are no mountains, no cities beyond the capital, and each resort occupies its own island. The landscape is water — lagoons in every shade of blue, reefs and sandbanks — and the experience is about privacy and the ocean.

Mauritius is a single volcanic island, ringed almost entirely by coral reef that creates sheltered lagoons. Inland rise jagged peaks, sugarcane fields, gorges and waterfalls. Its culture blends Indian, African, French, British and Chinese influences, expressed in food, temples, music and language. It feels lived-in and varied.

The Seychelles is an archipelago of granite and coral islands off East Africa. The inner granite islands — Mahé, Praslin and La Digue — are famous for sculpted boulders framing white beaches, forested hills and endemic wildlife, from giant tortoises to rare birds. Further out, coral islands host exclusive private-island resorts. It feels wild and cinematic.

What do you actually do all day?

This question decides more honeymoons than the photos do.

  • In the Maldives, days revolve around the water: snorkeling the house reef, diving with mantas, sandbank picnics, sunset cruises, spa treatments and long meals. There is little to explore on land. Couples who want to switch off completely love it; restless travelers may find a week enough.
  • In Mauritius, you can mix beach days with hikes in Black River Gorges National Park, the layered colors of Chamarel, the sacred lake at Grand Bassin, the markets of Port Louis, catamaran trips to the east-coast islets, golf, kitesurfing and an excellent food culture. It suits couples who like a full itinerary.
  • In the Seychelles, days include island-hopping by boat, cycling La Digue's quiet roads, walking among the coco de mer palms of Vallée de Mai on Praslin, nature trails, snorkeling and long hours on beaches that often feel empty. It suits nature lovers and photographers.

The Maldives for a honeymoon

The Maldives is the archetypal honeymoon destination for a reason: the overwater villa with steps into the lagoon, private pools, glass-floored decks and the sense of having an island to yourselves. Service is highly personal, often with a dedicated host, and resorts specialize in romantic touches — private dinners on the sand, couples' spa rituals and stargazing.

Choosing well means thinking about the atoll and transfer. Resorts near the capital are reached by speedboat at any hour; further atolls require a seaplane, which flies only in daylight, or a domestic flight and boat. A strong house reef lets you snorkel with turtles and reef sharks from your doorstep.

The considerations: most meals are taken at your resort, so dining variety depends on the island, and there is little culture or landscape beyond the water. Five to seven nights is the classic length; pairing the Maldives with a more varied destination before or after is a popular way to balance a longer honeymoon.

Mauritius for a honeymoon

Mauritius suits couples who want luxury with range. Resorts are typically large and polished, set on lagoon beaches with several restaurants, spas, water sports and, often, golf. Because the island is easy to explore by car with a driver, you can spend one day on a catamaran and the next in the mountains or on a food tour.

Where you stay shapes the experience:

  • The east coast faces the trade winds, keeping it breezy, with long beaches, lagoons and islets offshore.
  • The west coast is sheltered, drier and known for sunsets, dolphin-watching off Tamarin and the dramatic Le Morne Brabant, a UNESCO-listed mountain with a powerful history as a refuge for escaped slaves.
  • The north is lively, with calm water and plenty of dining.
  • The wild south is rugged and dramatic, with cliffs and fewer resorts.

English and French are both widely spoken, and Mauritian food — curries, rougailles, street snacks — is a genuine highlight.

The Seychelles for a honeymoon

The Seychelles appeals to couples who want raw natural beauty and a sense of discovery. Its signature image — powder-white sand framed by giant granite boulders — is found at beaches such as Anse Source d'Argent on La Digue and Anse Lazio on Praslin.

A classic honeymoon combines two or three islands:

  • Mahé, the main island, with the international airport, mountainous forest trails and a range of luxury resorts.
  • Praslin, home to the UNESCO-listed Vallée de Mai forest, where the coco de mer palm grows, and some of the archipelago's most celebrated beaches.
  • La Digue, small and slow, where bicycles are the main way to get around.
  • Private and outer islands, reached by small plane or helicopter, where a single resort may occupy the entire island.

Inter-island travel is by ferry, small plane or helicopter. The Seychelles rewards those who enjoy exploring; luxury here feels less about the room and more about where it puts you.

Flights and getting there

All three are long-haul destinations for most travelers from Europe, the Americas and Asia, and logistics vary.

  • The Maldives is reached via its international airport near the capital, Malé, with connections through Gulf, Asian and European hubs. Your onward resort transfer — speedboat, seaplane or domestic flight — must be factored into arrival times.
  • Mauritius has well-established links with Europe, including direct routes, as well as connections via the Gulf, Africa and Asia. Once you land, every resort is reached by road.
  • The Seychelles is reached via Mahé's international airport, usually through a Gulf or African hub. Inter-island ferries and flights then take you onward.

For couples traveling from North America, all three involve at least one connection. Arriving overnight and building in a buffer day softens jet lag. If your heart is set on a seaplane resort in the Maldives, confirm your flight lands in time for daylight transfers.

Can you combine two islands?

Yes — and on a honeymoon of ten nights or more, it can be the best of both. The most popular pairings are:

  • Seychelles + Maldives: nature and exploration on the granite islands, followed by pure relaxation in an overwater villa.
  • Mauritius + Maldives: culture, food and activity first, then reef and seclusion.
  • Mauritius + Réunion or a safari in East or Southern Africa + Seychelles or Mauritius: combinations that make use of regional flights.

The key is routing. Direct flights between the islands are limited and change by season, so connecting through a Gulf hub can be simpler than it looks. Plan the order around the flights, not the other way round, and aim for at least four nights in each place so the second island does not feel like a transit stop. A good travel planner or the resorts themselves can help coordinate connections.

When to go: seasons for each island

  • Maldives: the northeast monsoon, roughly December to April, is the dry season with calm seas. The southwest monsoon, roughly May to November, brings more showers and wind but also manta season in Baa Atoll and quieter resorts.
  • Mauritius: in the Southern Hemisphere, its seasons are reversed. May to December is cooler and drier, with September to November particularly pleasant. January to March is hot and humid, with a risk of tropical cyclones. The east coast is windier in the southern winter; the west coast stays sheltered.
  • Seychelles: warm all year. The northwest trade winds, roughly December to March, bring humidity and more rain; the southeast trades, roughly May to September, are drier but breezier, with seaweed affecting some beaches. The calm transition months of April–May and October–November are ideal for snorkeling and diving.

For a combined trip, the overlap of April and November works well for all three.

❓ Frequently asked questions

Which is better for a honeymoon: Maldives, Mauritius or Seychelles?

Choose the Maldives for overwater villas, reefs and complete relaxation; Mauritius for variety, with beaches, mountains, golf and culture; and the Seychelles for dramatic landscapes and island-hopping. Couples who want to do nothing beautifully choose the Maldives, while those who like exploring lean toward Mauritius or the Seychelles.

Is Mauritius or the Maldives better?

They offer different experiences. The Maldives is about water, privacy and overwater villas on single-resort islands. Mauritius is a larger volcanic island with lagoon beaches plus hiking, food, culture and excursions. Choose the Maldives to switch off completely and Mauritius if you want more to see and do.

When is the best time to visit the Seychelles?

The Seychelles is warm all year, but the transition months of April to May and October to November usually bring the calmest seas and best visibility. December to March is more humid and rainy, while May to September is drier but windier, with seaweed on some beaches.

When is cyclone season in Mauritius?

Mauritius's cyclone risk is highest during its hot, humid summer, roughly January to March. Cyclones do not strike every year, but weather can disrupt plans. The cooler, drier months from May to December are generally the most reliable time to visit.

Can you combine the Maldives and Seychelles in one trip?

Yes, it is a popular pairing: explore the Seychelles' granite islands first, then relax in a Maldives overwater villa. Direct flights between the two are limited and vary by season, so many itineraries connect via a Gulf hub. Allow at least four nights in each destination.

How many days do you need in the Seychelles?

Seven to ten nights allows two or three islands, typically Mahé and Praslin with a day trip or stay on La Digue. Island-hopping takes time, so avoid changing hotels too often. Add a few nights on a private outer island for a longer honeymoon.

Which Indian Ocean island has the best beaches?

It depends on your taste. The Maldives has sandbanks and lagoons of extraordinary clarity; the Seychelles has boulder-framed beaches such as Anse Source d'Argent; Mauritius has long lagoon beaches protected by reef. For variety of beach and scenery, the Seychelles is hard to beat.
