Why Tokyo for a Ultra-Luxury trip

At the very top, Tokyo luxury is about height, space and access. Expect corner suites with floor-to-ceiling glass, private dining rooms, butlers who can secure counter seats that are otherwise booked solid, and cars waiting at the curb for gallery visits in Roppongi or after-hours shopping in Ginza. Look for hotels occupying the upper floors of Marunouchi and Toranomon towers, where lobbies sit dozens of stories up and the city noise never reaches the room.

Tokyo does city luxury with unusual precision: hotel lobbies on the top floors of towers, service that anticipates rather than reacts, and a dining scene that runs from ten-seat sushi counters to formal French. The best stays pair skyline views of the Imperial Palace gardens or Mount Fuji on clear days with immediate access to Ginza, Marunouchi and Roppongi.

When to go: Late March to early April brings cherry blossoms along the moats and rivers, while October and November offer crisp, dry air and the clearest views of Mount Fuji. June is the rainy season and July through August is hot and humid.

MarunouchiFacing the Imperial Palace and Tokyo Station, it suits travelers who want calm, polished streets, palace-moat jogging paths and fast bullet-train connections.

Roppongi and ToranomonHigh-rise hotels here deliver sweeping night views, contemporary art museums and a lively bar scene for guests who like an after-dark city.
