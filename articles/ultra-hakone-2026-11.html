Why Hakone for a Ultra-Luxury trip

At its highest level, Hakone offers detached suites and villas with private onsen, generous terraces and complete seclusion in the forest. Look for properties with only a handful of rooms, personal attendants for each guest, and kaiseki menus built around local mountain vegetables and fish from nearby Sagami Bay. Chauffeured transfers from Tokyo and private guides for the region's museums and shrines complete the experience.

Hakone is Japan's classic hot-spring retreat, a volcanic mountain landscape within easy reach of Tokyo by direct train from Shinjuku. Luxury here means a ryokan or resort with private open-air baths, multi-course kaiseki dinners and, on clear days, views of Mount Fuji across Lake Ashi. It does slow, restorative mountain luxury better than almost anywhere in Japan.

When to go: November brings vivid autumn foliage, and spring is mild and green. Winter offers the clearest Mount Fuji views and the most atmospheric outdoor bathing, while June is the rainy season and summer is cooler than Tokyo.

GoraSet on a hillside at the top of the switchback railway, it is a convenient base for the Hakone Open-Air Museum, the ropeway and many upscale ryokan.
Lake AshiThe lakeshore near Moto-Hakone offers Mount Fuji views, the waterside torii gate of Hakone Shrine and a quieter, more scenic stay.
SengokuharaA highland plateau known for its golden pampas grass in autumn and its museums, it suits travelers who want open landscapes and space.
